Why there's no first or last light in — At high latitudes in summer, the sun's arc through the sky becomes so shallow that even at its lowest point – around midnight – it never dips far enough below the horizon for the sky to stop glowing. The threshold for civil twilight is 6° below the horizon. In some cities, the sun never reaches it, so the sky stays bright all night and true first or last light never arrives.

... On the equator, the sun moves at a steep angle and crosses the horizon quickly. As you move further north, its path becomes shallower, so it crosses the horizon more slowly – extending light before sunrise and after sunset.
